\section{Methodology}



\subsection{Stiffness Characterisation}
To characterise the stiffness profile, a linear stage was used with a load cell (brand) held in a clamp (SmallRig Magic Arm). This was attached to the end of the polyimide sleeve which was clamped in a 3D printed stand. The whole experimental setup was attached to a ThorLabs Breadboard. The linear stage was set to the resting length of the actuator (120mm) and the load cell was tared. The linear stage was set to compress the actuator to 30mm this corresponded to 0mm on the linear stage. The load cell was set to record and the linear stage was controlled to 150mm (sleeve length 180mm) at a speed of 0.5mm s$^{-1}$. The speed of the actuator was translated into the displacement using: $v= \frac{d}{t}$. The load cell data was collected using DSCUSB Toolkit and processed in Python.
 

\subsubsection{Temperature Stability of the Polyimide Sleeve}

Using the same experimental set-up as the stiffness characterisation. The polyimide sleeve was placed in an oven at 200$\degree$C for 1 hour, left to cool and then compressed and stretched as before. This was repeated 3 times before data was proccessed and plotted in Python. 

\subsection{Workspace Characterisation}

To characterise the usable workspace of the device, an experiment was set-up with the four camera Optitrack system and Motive 2.3 software to track the tip position of the device in 3D space with submillimeter accuracy (mean error: value). Two trajectories were examined, a uniaxial characterisation with the actuation of each tendon separately and a multiaxial characterisation through cyclical actuation of each tendon. This was completed for 4 different assemblies of actuator, the silicon actuator alone, the silicon actuator with the polyimide sleeve and both of these with a NiTi (0.6mm diameter) wire inserted through the central lumen. This was completed for three different lengths of actuator, 30mm, 60mm and 90mm. The 3D-printed tip with cable gland was attached to the end of the device to hold a retroflective ball that the Optitrack cameras could track. A size exclusion filter was set to only track the tip-mounted ball. Data was processed and plotted in Python


\subsection{Moulding of Polyamic Acid}
Polyamic acid  in N-methyl pyrollidone ( stats) was poured into a conical mould and heated to 150$\degree$C until thermal imidisation occured. This was attempted without removing the N-methyl pyrollidone (NMP) from the sample and also attmepted after drying the sample for 1 week with a SmartVap. Without removing NMP, the heating caused a yellow powder to be formed after 20 minutes. The dried sample formed a continous solid layer after 1 hour of heating. The solid layer occured at the circular base of the cone and broke into pieces (approx. 2mm$^3$) at the vertex.
