\section{Aims and Objectives}
The aim of this project was to investigate the use of a self-healing polyimide in a soft robotics context.

It was hypothesised that the incorporation of a S-H polyimide into soft robotic components would enhance their lifespan, increasing long term functionality and enabling real-world deployment in harsh environments.


The objectives of this project were to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Investigate the alteration of a polyimide through a Diels-Alder pathway to achieve a self-healing polymer 
    \item Investigate the processability of the modified polymer through material tests such as melting point analysis. 
    \item Based on the processability, apply the polymer to either a sheet film material or a moulded component suitable for use as a soft robotics component
    \item Based upon the form of the modified polymer, construct and use as an inflatable soft continuum robot or use as hard or soft bodied components 
    \item Test component/robot through different techniques such as tensile testing and force characterisation.
    \item If time allows, design and construct a system that can autonomously self heal components. For example, using a UV controlled self healing reaction, the flow rate of air into an inflatable device could be measured. Therefore meaning that if the flow rate changed a UV light could be activated, thereby autonomously self-healing the components.
\end{itemize}

This report is presented in a split-approach in order to cover the objectives in full detail. Firstly, chemical modifications are attempted and analysed in chapter \ref{cha:chem} before robotic contributions are covered in chapter \ref{cha:storm}.
