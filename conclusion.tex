\chapter{Conclusion} % enter the name of the chapter here
\label{cha:conclusion}

This report showed initial steps towards the development of a self healing polyimide, this was attempted through hydrolysis of a polyimide to a precursor, polyamic acid, and subsequent crosslink formation between chains of this precursor. The success of hydrolysis attempts were minimal, attributed here to a low concetration of alkaline reagent used. Crosslinking of polyamic acid was also unsuccesful. Here this was attributed to a highly competitive intramolecular imide formation blocking the attachment of a crosslinking reagent. An unexpected result discovered during catalytic crosslink attempts was a reduction in temperature required for thermal imidisation of polyamic acid to polyimide. This has potential to provide a greener production process of polyimide due to a 40$\degree$C decrease in temperature.

Other experiments performed with the same material had a soft-robotic focus. This involved the design and construction of an actuator shown to withstand temperatures of 200$\degree$C with no loss in capability. This device had a greater than hemispherical workspace with a maximum radius of 120mm. Follow-the-leader navigation with this device was demonstrated to x mm achieving a maximum tip angle of x$\degree$. Additional capabilities of this device were also shown such as targeted drug delivery in a stomach based simulation.

In the future, the chemical contributions to the robotics field will become more apparent. When crosslinking attempts become successful, a shift in reagents can make way for \textit{dynamic} Diels-Alder groups in order to re-bind chains that have been separated due to damage. This highly functional material can replace the current protective sleeve shown in this work and extend the lifetime of soft-robotic actuators.
